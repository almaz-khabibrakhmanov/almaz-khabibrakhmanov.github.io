Noncovalent interactions are of paramount importance to chemistry, materials science and biology. Yet most practical simulations still rely on simplistic models, which often fail to capture these interactions accurately in systems of modern interest. My work approaches this challenge from a single angle: build a minimal quantum model of how electrons respond, ask which physical effects really have to be accounted for, and turn the answer into potentials that survive in production simulations. The directions below follow that thread — from the coarse-grained model at its core, through the physics it exposes, to where it runs at scale.
