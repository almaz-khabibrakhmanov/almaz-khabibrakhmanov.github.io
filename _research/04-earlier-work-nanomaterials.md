Before moving to noncovalent interactions, I worked on the electronic and
mechanical properties of nanomaterials in Moscow. I showed that ultrasmall
diamond nanoparticles can be *stiffer than bulk diamond* depending on their size
and shape, and proposed an unambiguous definition of incompressibility at the
nanoscale. Separately, I modeled graphene oxide patterned by pristine graphene
nanoroads and computed the resulting electronic structure. Several of these projects
provided the theoretical counterpart to experimental studies, including h-BN/Al
nanocomposite UV photodetectors.

This is not my current direction, but it is where I learned solid-state physics, density-functional
theory in practice, and how to work with experimentalists. And who knows, maybe one day I will revisit
these topics — with a broader perspective and fresh ideas.
